Support writing · Reusable template

Make a support reply clear and useful.

A good support reply acknowledges the customer, states only what is known, and gives one safe next step. This structure works for a delayed order, a product question, or a request that needs investigation.

A five-part reply

  1. Acknowledge: name the issue in neutral words.
  2. Show understanding: recognize the impact without exaggerating.
  3. Share verified facts: distinguish confirmed status from what you are checking.
  4. Give the next step: say who will do what and when you will follow up.
  5. Close simply: invite a reply if the situation changes or the answer misses the mark.

Copy and adapt

Hi [Name],

Thanks for letting us know about [issue]. I understand this has affected [specific impact].

Here is what I can confirm: [verified fact]. I am checking [open question] and will update you by [realistic time and time zone].

For now, please [safe next step]. If anything changes, reply here and I will review it.

Best,
[Name / team]

Before you send

  • Do not promise a refund, replacement, or delivery date unless policy and records confirm it.
  • Do not include another customer's details or ask for passwords or full payment-card numbers.
  • Use an approved channel for identity checks and account changes.
  • Remove internal notes and placeholders from the final message.

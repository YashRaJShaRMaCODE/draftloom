Write client emails people can act on.

A clear client email makes three things easy to find: why you are writing, what the reader needs to know, and what should happen next. Use this checklist before you send a project update, question, or request.

1. Put the point near the top

Start with the status or request, not a long warm-up. “The first draft is ready for review” gives the reader a useful anchor before you add background.

2. Keep facts separate from decisions

State what is known, what is still uncertain, and what you need the client to decide. Do not turn an estimate, assumption, or possible date into a promise.

3. Make the next step specific

Replace “let me know your thoughts” with a clear action and a reasonable date: “Could you approve the headline or leave comments by Thursday?” Include the time zone when timing matters.

4. Use one main request per paragraph

Short paragraphs help a reader scan on a phone. Put secondary questions in bullets, and keep unrelated topics in a separate email or clearly labeled section.

5. Review names, links, and commitments

Before sending, confirm the recipient, attachments, dates, figures, and any statement about scope or delivery. A writing tool can improve wording; it cannot verify your project facts.

A reusable outline

Hi [Name],

[Main point or current status.]

[One short detail the reader needs.]

Could you [specific next step] by [date and time zone]?

Best,
[Your name]

Adapt the outline to the relationship and the actual agreement. Do not add a deadline or commitment that the client has not accepted.
